Is EditPad Lite free? Personal use versus the paid licence

Is EditPad Lite free? Yes, for personal use. The vendor allows home and school use that earns no income at no charge, and asks for a paid licence as soon as the editor is used for a job or a business.

EditPad Lite is free for personal use only, which the vendor defines as use at home or school that does not generate income. No payment or registration is needed for that. Use for work or business requires a licence from 24.95 euro per user, which also adds a spell checker, forum access and support for files over 2 GB.

The rule the vendor sets

Is EditPad Lite free to use? The answer depends on where you use it. The vendor's download and upgrade pages say the same thing: EditPad Lite is free for personal use only. Personal means at home or at school, in a way that does not earn money.

For that use you download the installer, run it and start typing. There is no payment, no registration and no key. The pages checked for this guide mention no time limit and no trial period for personal use.

Work is different. Use for a job or a business requires a purchased licence. The vendor describes the free download as freeware for non commercial use at home or school, and treats the paid licence as the version for everything else.

Which side of the line you are on

The vendor does not publish a longer definition than this, so borderline cases such as volunteer work for a charity are not addressed on its pages.

  • Writing notes, letters or personal code at home: free.
  • Students doing schoolwork: free, as long as the work earns no income.
  • Editing files as part of a paid job, on any computer: licence required.
  • A business installing it for staff: licence required, counted by users. See pricing for the tiers.
  • Paid freelance work at home: the vendor's test is whether the use generates income, so this points to a licence. Ask the vendor if your case is unclear.

Moving from free to licensed

Buying a licence does not mean starting again. After checkout the vendor sends a user ID, and the purchased download section of its download page provides the licensed build, listed at 19 MB against 18 MB for the free one. One user ID covers every language edition of version 8.

A single user licence covers all the computers one person uses, so the same licence serves a desktop and a laptop. If the licensed copy disappoints, the three month money back guarantee applies.

When neither edition fits

Some needs are outside Lite altogether, paid or not. Column selections, sorting lines, recording macros, comparing two files and editing files larger than memory are EditPad Pro features. The EditPad Lite versus EditPad Pro comparison shows the split.

Either way the starting point is the same vendor site. Check the supported Windows versions, then get the file through the download page.

Frequently asked questions

Is EditPad Lite really free?

Yes, for personal use. The vendor states that home or school use that does not generate income needs no payment and no registration. It is not free for work or business use, which requires a licence.

Can I use EditPad Lite at work for free?

No. The vendor's terms say EditPad Lite is free for personal use only, and that use for a job or a business requires a purchased licence. A single user licence costs 24.95 euro before tax.

Does the free version of EditPad Lite expire?

The vendor's pages mention no time limit or trial period for personal use. The free edition is described as freeware for non commercial use at home or school, with no registration.

What does the paid EditPad Lite licence add?

A licence permits work and business use. According to the vendor it also adds an integrated spell checker, access to the user forum, the Syntax Coloring Scheme Editor download, and editing of files larger than 2 GB that fit in available memory.

Do I have to register the free version?

No. The vendor states that the free version needs no payment or registration. Only buyers receive a user ID, which is used for the purchased download.

Is EditPad Lite a trial of EditPad Pro?

No. EditPad Lite is a separate, smaller edition that stays free for personal use. EditPad Pro has its own free evaluation download, which the vendor says reminds you to buy a licence if you keep using it.
